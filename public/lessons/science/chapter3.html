Food Diversity in India

Food eaten in India varies greatly across states, influenced by locally grown crops, cultural preferences, and traditions. For example, Punjab has wheat and chickpea with foods like Makki di roti, while Karnataka features rice and ragi with idli and dosa.

“Food gives life to living beings.” – Sanskrit Saying

Traditional and modern cooking tools have evolved from chulha and sil-batta to gas stoves and electrical grinders, influenced by technology and lifestyle changes.

Food Components & Nutrients

Our food contains key nutrients: carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins, minerals, dietary fibers, and water. Carbohydrates and fats give energy; proteins help build and repair the body.

  • Carbohydrates: Wheat, rice, banana, potato
  • Fats: Nuts, seeds, ghee, butter
  • Proteins: Milk, pulses, eggs, fish
  • Vitamins & Minerals: Protect the body and prevent diseases
  • Dietary Fibres: Aid digestion, found in fruits, vegetables, whole grains

Testing Food Components

We can detect key nutrients in food by simple tests:

  • Starch Test: Food turns blue-black with iodine (e.g., potato, rice)
  • Fat Test: Oily patch on paper indicates fat content (e.g., butter, peanuts)
  • Protein Test: Violet color with copper sulfate and caustic soda (e.g., peas, bread)

Caution: Some tests require chemicals and safety measures.

Balanced Diet & Junk Food

A balanced diet includes all essential nutrients in right amounts along with adequate fibers and water. Junk food is high in sugars and fats but low in vitamins and fiber and can cause health problems like obesity.

Compare nutritional values: Of potato wafers (high fats and calories) vs roasted chana (higher protein and fiber).

Remember: Good health is the ultimate wealth.

Millets & Food Miles

Millets like jowar, bajra, and ragi are native, nutritious grains rich in vitamins, minerals, and fibers, easily cultivated in varied climates and called nutri-cereals.

Food miles refer to the distance food travels from farm to plate. Minimizing food miles by eating local produce supports farmers, reduces pollution, and keeps food fresh.

Take only what you can eat to avoid wastage and respect the effort behind every meal.
